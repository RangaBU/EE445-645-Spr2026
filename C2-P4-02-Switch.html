Figure Caption
Two equivalent ways to compute the differential scattering coefficient σs of a vegetation canopy modeled as a turbid medium. Left panel shows the direct approach: each of the N leaf elements (areas s1, s2, …, sN) in the elementary volume is individually illuminated along the incident direction Ω′. Each leaf i with normal ΩLi contributes a projected area si |ΩLi · Ω′| weighted by its individual scattering phase function γLi(Ω′ → Ω; ΩLi) dΩ, which describes the angular redistribution into a differential solid angle about the scattered direction Ω. This is exact but impractical when N is large. Right panel shows the efficient alternative: the upper hemisphere of leaf normal directions is partitioned into M solid angle bins. Replacing the individual leaf areas with the leaf angle pdf gL(ΩL) converts the discrete sum into a continuous integral over the hemisphere, absorbing the individual phase functions γLi into the area scattering phase function (1/π) Γ(Ω′ → Ω). This yields the differential scattering coefficient σs = uL · (1/π) Γ, where uL is the leaf area density (Ross, 1981).
